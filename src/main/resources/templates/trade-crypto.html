What is cryptocurrency trading?

Trading means buying and selling coins on an exchange, hoping to sell for more than you paid. It is a fast-moving, risky activity: prices can swing 10% or more in a day, and most short-term traders lose money. Learn how it works before you ever put in more than you can afford to lose.

Where do crypto prices come from?

There is no single official price. Every exchange runs its own order book where buyers and sellers post bids and asks; the last matched trade sets that exchange's price. Sites such as CoinGecko average the prices across many exchanges, which is what our Charts page shows.

What are crypto price charts? See live charts

A price chart plots how the price moved over time. Candlestick charts show, for each interval, where the price opened, closed, and its high and low. Charts help you see trends and volatility, but past movement never guarantees where the price will go next.

Understanding crypto trading volume

Volume is the total amount traded in a period. High volume means many participants and usually tighter spreads, so you can buy or sell close to the quoted price. Very low volume is a warning sign: prices can be moved by a single large order.

What is technical analysis?

Technical analysis studies charts, patterns and indicators such as moving averages to guess where the price might head. Some traders find it useful for timing; academic evidence for its predictive power is weak. Treat it as one tool, not a crystal ball.

Simple trading strategies

Beginners usually do best with the simplest approaches: buy a fixed amount at regular intervals (dollar-cost averaging), decide in advance when you would sell, never trade with borrowed money, and keep coins you plan to hold long-term in your own wallet rather than on an exchange.
